# Akizen Portfolio — Demo Project 2

## RONA Auto Studio — Complete Concept Project Brief

> **Concept Project / Demo Build**
>
> RONA Auto Studio is a fictional Malaysian business created solely as an Akizen portfolio demonstration. It is not an Akizen client. Any business details, pricing, imagery, service descriptions, reviews, addresses, operating hours or other commercial information used in the final demo must be treated as fictional concept content unless explicitly sourced and identified otherwise. No real client relationship, business result, testimonial, revenue figure or conversion statistic may be implied.

## 1. Fictional business name

**RONA Auto Studio**

Descriptor: **Detailing · Paint Correction · Protection**

Suggested short brand line: **Finish worth looking back at.**

“Rona” is a Malay word associated with appearance, colour and visual character. It makes the brand feel locally grounded without becoming traditional or generic, while still working naturally for a premium automotive-care studio.

---

## 2. Business type

A **premium automotive detailing and paint-protection studio in Ipoh, Perak**, serving daily drivers, enthusiast cars and newer premium vehicles.

Core services:

- Exterior detailing
- Interior detailing
- Paint correction
- Ceramic coating
- New-car protection packages
- Maintenance detailing

The business is deliberately not positioned as a mechanical workshop, car wash chain or supercar-only boutique.

## 3. Why this industry was selected

Automotive detailing creates the strongest contrast with Demo Project 1.

Demo 1 can prove Akizen understands professional-services credibility, restraint, hierarchy and trust. Demo 2 should prove Akizen can also create a visually expressive, conversion-focused local-business website where photography, mobile behaviour and direct-response UX do most of the selling.

The category gives Akizen room to demonstrate:

1. High-impact visual art direction without becoming a technology-style website.
2. Before/after presentation that immediately communicates value.
3. Strong mobile conversion through WhatsApp and slot enquiries.
4. Clear service/package comparison for a purchase people often find confusing.
5. Location, operating-hour and local-trust UX that ordinary Malaysian small businesses immediately recognise.

A detailing studio is also commercially believable as the kind of RM-scale website project a small Malaysian studio could realistically sell and deliver.

---

## 4. Business backstory

RONA Auto Studio is a fictional independent detailing studio created around a simple problem: many owners want their cars to look properly cared for, but the detailing market can be difficult to understand.

Customers encounter overlapping package names, vague coating claims and pricing that is impossible to compare. RONA therefore positions itself around **clear recommendations, visible workmanship and care appropriate to the actual condition of the car**.

The studio serves customers around Ipoh from a fictional Greentown-area location. Its visual identity is premium but not intimidating: the business should feel equally comfortable detailing a well-kept Honda Civic, Mazda CX-5, Toyota Harrier or BMW 3 Series.

Do not invent an establishment year, founder biography, certification, award or number of completed cars unless it is clearly labelled fictional concept copy.

## 5. Target customers

### Primary customer

**Age:** roughly 27–45

**Location:** Ipoh and nearby areas in Perak

**Vehicle profile:** newer daily drivers, enthusiast-owned vehicles, family SUVs, premium sedans and recently purchased used cars

**Device behaviour:** predominantly mobile; discovery may come from Instagram, TikTok, Google Maps, Facebook, a shared WhatsApp link or a Google search

**Typical intent:**

- “My paint looks dull/swirl-marked.”
- “I just bought a new car and want to protect it.”
- “The interior needs a proper clean.”
- “I want to know what ceramic coating actually includes.”
- “How much, how long, and do I need an appointment?”

### Trust concerns

Customers worry about:

- unclear package differences
- hidden/add-on pricing
- damage caused by poor polishing technique
- exaggerated coating promises
- whether photographs actually represent the shop’s work
- how long the car will be unavailable
- whether the service is appropriate for their particular paint condition

### Purchase behaviour

The website rarely needs to close the full sale by itself. Its job is to make the customer confident enough to **send a useful WhatsApp enquiry** and reduce the number of vague “price?” messages.

## 7. Brand personality

RONA should feel:

- **Precise** — workmanship is shown in surfaces, lighting and close-up details.
- **Confident** — short copy, decisive hierarchy, no desperate sales language.
- **Obsessive in a useful way** — attention to paint, trim, texture and finish.
- **Approachable** — premium presentation without making normal-car owners feel unwelcome.

## 11. Full content strategy

### Content job 1 — Translate detailing jargon into customer problems

Lead with outcomes/problems before technical service names.

Instead of:

> Stage 1 polish

Use:

> **Bring back clarity and gloss**
> A refinement treatment for paint that looks hazy or lightly swirled under the sun.

The technical label can appear secondarily.

### Content job 2 — Set realistic expectations

Credibility comes from what RONA refuses to overpromise.

Examples:

- “Some deeper scratches require paintwork rather than polishing.”
- “Ceramic coating helps with maintenance and chemical resistance; it does not make paint scratch-proof.”
- “Final recommendation depends on paint condition and vehicle size.”

### Content job 3 — Make price and time visible

Show “from” pricing and indicative time where commercially sensible. Always pair it with the factors that change the final quote.

### Content job 4 — Use images as evidence

Every key claim should have a visual counterpart:

- gloss → reflection/detail image
- correction → before/after
- interior reset → texture/crevice/detail image
- coating → hydrophobic/wash-maintenance imagery
- process → inspection light, masking, polishing and handover shots

### Content job 5 — Reduce WhatsApp friction

Every major service area should provide a contextual CTA. The site should pre-answer “what should I send you?” with vehicle model, concern and preferred date.

### Voice

Short, specific, composed, non-salesy Malaysian English. Avoid slang in core interface copy. Bahasa Malaysia can be used later as a parallel version, but the initial portfolio implementation should remain a coherent English-first experience rather than mixing languages randomly.

## 13. Visual identity

### Brand idea

**Controlled reflections.**

The visual system should be built around how automotive paint behaves under inspection lighting: hard highlights, deep dark surfaces, precise lines and moments of warm reflected colour.

### Logo direction

A clean custom wordmark reading **RONA**, preferably wide and slightly condensed. Avoid shields, wings, piston icons, car silhouettes, crowns or generic detailing badges.

A secondary mark can use a simple **R/** monogram or a sliced horizontal line derived from a reflection highlight.

### Graphic language

- thin technical rules
- crop marks used sparingly
- oversized service numerals
- reflective highlight lines
- deliberate asymmetry
- large photographic fields
- restrained labels in uppercase

Do not turn the site into a “HUD” or dashboard interface.

---

## 14. Colour direction

Use a small controlled palette.

### Primary

**Obsidian** — `#101112`  
Main dark surfaces; slightly softer than pure black.

**Warm Pearl** — `#F2EFE8`  
Primary light background/text counterpart; avoids clinical white.

### Secondary

**Graphite** — `#242628`  
Panels, borders, secondary dark regions.

**Fog** — `#B9B7B0`  
Secondary text and neutral UI.

### Accent

**Burnished Copper** — `#C46F3D`  
Use for active states, small labels, CTA moments and photographic accent coordination.

The copper should occupy roughly 5–10% of the visual field. It is a signal, not a background colour.

### Rules

- No rainbow gradients.
- No neon blue/purple tech gradient.
- Avoid pure black everywhere; allow tonal depth.
- Let photography provide most of the colour variation.

---

## 15. Typography

### Display / headings

**Barlow Condensed** — SemiBold / Bold

Use for large headlines, service numerals and compact section statements. It supplies automotive energy without looking like a motorsport poster when spacing is controlled.

### Body / interface

**Manrope** — Regular / Medium / SemiBold

Use for navigation, body copy, buttons, price information and utility labels.

### Typesetting direction

- Desktop hero heading: approximately 72–104 px depending on viewport.
- Mobile hero heading: approximately 48–64 px.
- Headings should often be short enough to use 1–3 lines.
- Body width: 45–65 characters where possible.
- Tight display leading; generous body leading.
- Uppercase labels use tracking, but normal paragraph text does not.

Both fonts are available through Google Fonts and suitable for a static site.

## 16. Photography direction

Photography is the most important asset category in the project.

### Required visual set

1. **Hero exterior** — dark or metallic vehicle under controlled directional light, 3/4 crop, visible body-line reflection.
2. **Paint inspection** — close-up under inspection lamp showing swirls/clarity.
3. **Correction process** — polisher, masking, pad/paint interface, technician hands rather than posed staff portraits.
4. **Interior** — leather, stitching, controls, vents and high-touch areas.
5. **Protection/maintenance** — water behaviour, wash mitt, careful drying or coating application.
6. **Studio context** — clean bay, lighting, organised tools and vehicle scale.
7. **Handover/detail** — emblem, wheel, trim, mirror or rear quarter detail.

### Art-direction rules

- Prefer low-angle or tight editorial crops over wide generic workshop photos.
- Preserve dark shadow detail; avoid crushed blacks.
- Use practical lights/reflection bars in composition.
- Avoid licence plates where possible or replace/obscure them in concept assets.
- Avoid identifiable customers.
- Do not use random supercars for every image. A premium daily-driver mix makes the concept more believable to normal business owners.
- If stock photography is used, it must be consistent enough to feel like one studio and legally usable for the demo.

### Image treatment

Minimal grading. Slightly warm highlights, neutral paint colour, rich blacks. No heavy orange/teal cinema preset.

## 17. UI/component system

The system should be compact and reusable without making every section look like a card grid.

### Core components

**1. Header**  
Transparent over hero, transitioning to solid obsidian after scroll. Desktop nav + strong WhatsApp button. Mobile menu button and persistent conversion access.

**2. CTA button**  
Primary: Warm Pearl background on dark surfaces or Obsidian on light surfaces. Copper reserved for hover/focus marker, arrow or small accent rather than filling every button.

**3. Service pathway**  
Large text-led row with supporting image/crop and a directional arrow. On mobile, one item per row with strong tap area.

**4. Service price module**  
Service title, one-sentence “best for,” starting price, duration and CTA. Avoid generic icon cards.

**5. Before/after viewer**  
Accessible range/drag control with text labels. Provide fallback toggle for touch and reduced-motion users.

**6. Work gallery**  
Editorial masonry/asymmetric layout on desktop; swipeable snap carousel or stacked crop sequence on mobile.

**7. Process rail**  
Four numbered stages with a single connecting rule. Becomes vertical on mobile.

**8. FAQ accordion**  
Large readable tap targets and subtle motion.

**9. Contact strip**  
WhatsApp, call, directions and hours presented as actions, not a form-heavy contact section.

**10. Concept badge**  
Small persistent “Concept Project” cue in hero/footer and an explicit full disclaimer in footer/Studio page.

### Radius and borders

Use small-to-medium radii (roughly 4–12 px). Automotive precision is weakened by excessively bubbly UI. Use 1 px tonal borders sparingly.

## 18. Mobile-first UX

Mobile is the primary design target.

### Mobile priorities

1. **CTA always reachable.** Use a compact sticky bottom action bar after the hero with WhatsApp as primary and call as secondary.
2. **Decision before detail.** The first scroll should expose “What does your car need?” rather than a long brand story.
3. **Thumb-friendly service browsing.** Large rows, minimum 44 px targets, clear pricing and no hover-dependent information.
4. **Fast imagery.** Responsive `srcset`, modern formats, intentional crops and lazy loading below the fold. Do not sacrifice perceived quality, but do not ship desktop-sized images to phones.
5. **Low form burden.** Prefer WhatsApp-prefilled enquiry to an unnecessary multi-field booking form.

### Mobile hero

- Height around 85–95svh, not blindly 100vh.
- Subject placed so headline remains readable without a heavy opaque overlay.
- Headline first, CTA within initial viewport where practical.
- A subtle downward cue can reveal the service chooser.

### Mobile navigation

Menu items:

- Services
- Work
- Studio
- Contact

Persistent CTA: **WhatsApp**

### Sticky action bar

Appears after leaving hero and disappears near footer to avoid duplicate CTAs.

Actions:

- **WhatsApp**
- **Call**

## 19. Desktop presentation

Desktop should feel more editorial and immersive rather than simply stretching the mobile layout.

### Desktop characteristics

- Wide 12-column grid.
- Large image fields crossing columns.
- Short copy blocks aligned against vehicle details/reflections.
- Asymmetric service and work layouts.
- Generous horizontal whitespace around concise text.
- Sticky media or sticky copy only where it genuinely helps explain a service.

### Recommended hero composition

Left/bottom-aligned headline occupying roughly 5 columns; vehicle image spans the viewport. The headline may overlap a darker empty region of the photo, never the visually important body line.

### Recommended transformation composition

Split 7/5 columns: large interactive image on the left, explanatory copy and treatment summary on the right. At large sizes the image remains sticky for a short scroll sequence showing inspection → correction → finish.

### Recommended service presentation

Do not use a 3-column card row. Use three full-width editorial bands with alternating image position so the page has rhythm.

## 20. Motion and microinteraction direction

Motion should make surfaces feel tactile and intentional.

### Hero

Very subtle image scale from ~1.03 to 1.00 on load. Headline and CTA rise/fade with short stagger. No long cinematic intro that blocks interaction.

### Scroll

Image masks/reveals can move 8–16% slower than the viewport for depth. Avoid heavy scroll-jacking.

### Service rows

On hover: image crop shifts slightly, arrow advances 4–6 px, underline/rule grows. On touch: immediate active state; never rely on hover to reveal content.

### Before/after

Handle movement is direct and physical. No autoplay comparison animation after the user starts interacting.

### Gallery

Small scale or crop shift on hover; captions reveal through opacity/translation. Mobile uses native-feeling horizontal snapping where appropriate.

### Buttons

Short 140–220 ms transitions. A small arrow or edge accent moves; the button itself should not bounce excessively.

### Reduced motion

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and disable parallax/large transforms while keeping content fully visible.

## 21. Conversion strategy

### Primary funnel

**Visual impact → identify problem → understand treatment → see proof → see starting price/time → WhatsApp enquiry.**

### Conversion principles

**1. Use outcome-led navigation.**  
People should not need to know detailing terminology before choosing where to click.

**2. Put WhatsApp in context.**  
A CTA after paint correction should prefill a correction-related message; a CTA after interior detailing should prefill an interior-related message.

**3. Show enough pricing to qualify.**  
“From” pricing prevents wasted enquiries while preserving inspection-based quotes.

**4. Make local logistics visible.**  
Location, hours, approximate treatment duration and appointment expectations should be easy to find from any page.

**5. Build trust through process and limitations.**  
The most persuasive copy is often an honest sentence about what polishing/coating cannot do.

### CTA hierarchy

Primary: **Check a slot on WhatsApp**  
Secondary: **Explore services / View work**  
Tertiary: **Call / Directions**

### Avoid

- newsletter popups
- fake scarcity timers
- fake “5 people viewing” notices
- forced lead forms before pricing
- fake customer-review counts
- multiple floating buttons competing for attention
